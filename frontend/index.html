    Reading the signals

    Every number on this board measures opportunity, not past scoring. Here is what each one means and why it matters for waivers.

    Snap share

    Share of the team's offensive snaps the player was on the field for. Coaches play the players they trust; everything else follows snaps.

    Target share

    Share of the team's targets. The cleanest measure of a pass-catcher's role. Anything over 20% is a featured receiver.

    Touch share

    Share of the team's total touches, targets plus carries. For running backs this is backfield control.

    Air yards

    How far downfield the player's targets traveled, added up. Downfield targets carry more touchdown and big-play value.

    WOPR

    Weighted Opportunity Rating: 1.5 x target share + 0.7 x air-yard share. One number for how valuable a receiver's targets are.

    Red-zone touches

    Touches inside the opponent's 20-yard line. Touchdowns come from here, so this is where breakout scoring starts.

    Deep targets

    Targets traveling 20 or more yards in the air. Low odds on each one, high payoff; a sign of a growing downfield role.

    Breakout score

    0 to 100 ranking of how fast a player's usage is rising, built from touches, snaps, and targets. Higher means louder signal.
